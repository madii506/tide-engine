A new tide model: trade-indexed liquidity sealing, executed in depth-matched passes that deepen the pool with every trade.

Abstract

A tide is a coin whose fees are spent on its own depth. Every trade pays a creator fee; the fee buys the coin, pairs it with SOL, and adds both to the coin’s pool; the receipt for that liquidity is burned, so it can never be withdrawn. The water rises with every trade and never goes out. Buyback-and-burn shrinks supply. A tide also deepens the pool beneath it: every pass adds liquidity that no one, not even TIDE, can take back. This paper describes T.I.D.E., a Trade-Indexed Depth Engine. It claims continuously, spends only what it claimed, and sizes every pass to the depth of the pool itself, so a shallow pool is answered gently and a deep one with a larger pour. Depth becomes a function of trading rather than of anyone’s promise.

1The tide

Every trade of a pump.fun coin pays a small creator fee into a vault that belongs to whoever created the coin. Most creators treat it as income. A tide treats it as water. The fee is claimed; half of it buys the coin; the coin and the other half go into the pool together; and the LP tokens that represent that deposit are burned. That liquidity is now part of the pool for good. A deeper pool means each sell moves the price less and each buy fills more cleanly, and the next trade pays the next fee into the same machine.

Nothing in it requires a promise. It requires only that the machine runs, every time, for as long as the coin trades.

2How tides go out

Liquidity usually leaves the way it came. Almost every pool fails in one of four ways:

the pull
LP tokens sit in a wallet, and one transaction takes the pool away.
the shallows
A thin pool, where every sell is a crater and every buy a spike.
the lump
Fees pile up for days, then one large buy prints a candle, gets sandwiched, and retraces by evening.
the hand
Someone has to remember to claim. They stop remembering, or they keep the fees.

TIDE removes all four. It seals what it adds, pours only in proportion to depth, runs on a clock, and spends only what it claimed.

3Depth

Let P be the claimed fees not yet spent and Q the SOL side of the coin’s pool. Each pass pours one slice:

τ = min(P, max(τmin, Q / 200))(3.1)

with τmin = 0.01 SOL. Half of τ buys the coin and half is paired with it, so a pass moves the price by about half a percent however deep the pool is. In a shallow pool the slice is small; as the pool deepens, the slice grows with it. That is the compounding: the deeper the pool, the bigger the pour; the bigger the pour, the deeper the pool.

dL/dt ≥ 0(3.2)

Sealed liquidity L never decreases. A coin that trades deepens. A coin that stops trading stops deepening, but nothing it sealed ever leaves.

Before graduation there is no pool to seal into, so TIDE buys on the bonding curve and burns what it buys, sized the same way against the curve’s virtual SOL reserves.

4The machine

Every coin launched here is created on pump.fun with its creator set to a fresh wallet that only the engine controls. Every minute the engine visits each one and runs six steps:

Step · what happenslive pass
claim
Collects the creator fees pump.fun has set aside for the coin. Only what arrives is ever spent.
keep
Keeps 0.015 SOL in the coin’s own wallet so it can pay its own gas and rent.
size
Sizes this pass from the claimed fees: 0.5% of the pool’s SOL side, never below 0.01 SOL.
buy
Spends half of it on the coin, on the bonding curve or on PumpSwap once it has graduated.
pair
Adds the coin it bought and the other half in SOL to the PumpSwap pool.
seal
Burns the LP tokens it received. That liquidity can never be withdrawn.

The accounting is deliberately narrow. A claim is measured as the wallet’s balance after it minus the balance before it, and only that amount joins the pool. It never spends SOL it did not claim, and it burns only what its own transactions delivered.

5What it will not do

It will not sell. It will not withdraw. It cannot: the LP tokens are burned the moment they arrive. It will not spend the gas reserve, and it will not pour faster than the pool can hold.

The engine holds each coin’s wallet, so the rules are enforced by a process rather than by a contract. Everything it does is public: every claim, buy, pair and seal is recorded with its transaction on each tide’s page. If the engine stops, nothing is lost. Fees keep accumulating in pump.fun’s vault, and the next pass claims all of it at once.
